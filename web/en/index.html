A cleaner deletes and asks you to trust it. Brisa sets things aside. Everything it removes waits with its expiry date and goes back where it was inside the window you choose.

How all of it is kept

  1. It moves, it doesn't delete.

    The file leaves its folder and lands in Brisa Trash. Nothing is erased at that step.

  2. It waits with you.

    Seven, thirty or sixty days, with its expiry date in plain sight and its size next to it.

  3. It goes back where it was.

    One click and it returns to its exact path. Once the days are up, the trash empties itself.

The questions I get most.

What if it takes something I need?

Cleaning never deletes. What Brisa sets aside waits with its expiry date and comes back within the 7, 30 or 60 day window. Once that is up the trash is emptied, and then it is gone. Uninstalling is final, and it warns you first.

Why does macOS warn me the first time I open it?

Because Brisa carries no Apple developer certificate, and macOS only lets you open that way what it could not verify. It does not mean anything is wrong with Brisa, and the steps are on the download page.

How much does it cost?

Nothing. Brisa is free and complete, with no account, no subscription and no paid version.

Does Brisa delete anything on its own?

No. It scans first and shows you the plan, and nothing moves until you confirm. The one exception is automatic maintenance, if you turn it on.
