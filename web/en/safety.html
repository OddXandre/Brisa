Careful by default.

A cleaner goes into places macOS protects. This is exactly what Brisa does in each of them.

Three things, in this order.

  1. Remove only what is safe.

    Caches apps rebuild, downloads nobody opened, and repeated copies.

  2. Keeps what it removes for as long as you decide.

    What it removes waits in Brisa Trash with its expiry date showing, and the 7, 30 or 60 day window is yours to pick in Settings.

  3. It tells you everything it did.

    Every cleanup is logged, with what it freed and when.

Six rules, no exceptions.

No cleanup ever deletes anything. Everything goes through Brisa Trash. Kept for 7, 30 or 60 days, and it goes back where it was inside that window.reversible
Nothing is deleted without your say-so. You see the plan first. Then you decide.with your permission
Uninstalling cannot be undone, and it says so. Whatever the app left behind on the disk goes too.with a warning
Your things stay untouched. Photos, documents and music stay out. Always.untouched
No account, no network. Nothing is sent anywhere: the whole scan happens on your Mac.local
One permission, and you decide. Without it, less is measured, and with it everything is. You can withdraw it whenever you want.optional

What Brisa never touches.

Cleanup and maintenance use the same exclusion list.

  • Your photos and videos, and everything inside Photos.
  • Your documents, wherever they live, iCloud Drive included.
  • Your music and audio, including what you downloaded to listen offline.
  • Installed applications. Brisa never uninstalls anything on its own.
  • The system and its files.

One permission, and optional.

Brisa asks for nothing when it opens. Without Full Disk Access it measures everything else and says so in the summary, with a button that takes you to the setting.

With it, Brisa also measures Documents, Desktop, Downloads, Photos, iCloud, Mail and Messages. macOS grants it per version, so updating means repeating the step.

If you would rather not grant it, Brisa still works. It only misses the part of the disk macOS hides.
